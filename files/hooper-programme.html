HOOPER
PROGRAMME
Built for a schedule that's actually moving — Evri's gone, a new 10pm–6am night job is coming in, uni's got fixed slots, and basketball's probably a night thing too. So this isn't pinned to Mon/Tue/Wed anymore. It's a 4-day Upper/Lower rotation you slot into whatever week you actually get.
⚠️ HOW THIS FITS THE WEEK
Train the 4 days in order — Upper A → Lower A → Upper B → Lower B — then repeat. Don't chase fixed weekdays; chase the rotation. Wednesday's free most weeks, so that's a natural anchor — build the other 3 around whatever uni, the night job, and ball actually leave open that week.

Rule of thumb with the night shift: if a session's happening before you sleep for a 10pm start, keep it at 7/10 effort, not a max-out. Save the heaviest attempts (top sets, PR pushes) for a day you've slept a full night before.
🔵 WHAT THIS PROGRAMME IS ACTUALLY FOR
Two numbers to move: bench toward 100kg (last confirmed PR 85kg, so this rebuilds that number properly rather than assuming it's still there), and body comp toward 87–88kg in 3 months / a leaner ~80kg within the year, off a starting point of 91.8kg at 22.8% body fat. Training holds onto your 40.7kg of skeletal muscle while that happens — the Food Plan tool is what actually drives the fat loss, this just makes sure you don't lose muscle doing it. Vertical power work is built into both lower days for the dunk goal.

PROGRAMME NOTES
🔄
Why Upper/Lower instead of Push/Pull/Legs
The old version was built around a fixed Evri shift pattern that no longer exists — Push and Pull were split apart mainly to keep leg day away from a specific job's demands. That constraint is gone, and PPL's real cost is that it wants a stable weekly slot to work well. Upper/Lower in a 4-day rotation doesn't care what day it lands on — it just needs the days to go in order, so it fits a schedule that's still moving between a new night job, uni, and basketball.
📅
Why there's no weekday grid this time
Uni's only partly fixed right now (busy Monday and Tuesday, Wednesday free, a double session Thursday), the night job runs 10pm–6am and it's not yet clear which days ball lands on. Pinning sessions to specific weekdays would just get broken the first week something moves. Follow the rotation order instead — Upper A, Lower A, Upper B, Lower B, repeat — and slot each one into whatever gap that week actually has. Wednesday being free most weeks makes it a good default anchor.
🛏️
Training around a 10pm–6am shift
Whatever the new job turns out to involve, working through the night means your sleep is happening at an odd time relative to training. Keep sessions that fall in the few hours before a shift starts at 7/10 effort — controlled, not maxed. Save top-set attempts and PR pushes for a day you've had a proper night's sleep beforehand. This matters more on Lower B, the highest-demand session, than anywhere else.
🎯
The bench number, honestly
85kg was the last confirmed PR, but training's been inconsistent since, so treat that as history, not a floor. Upper A rebuilds it properly with a real 5×5 progression rather than assuming it's still there — once you're moving 85kg for clean 5×5, that's the real signal to start pushing toward 100kg, not before.
⚖️
Training vs. the weight goal
87–88kg in 3 months and a leaner ~80kg within the year is a Food Plan job, not a training job — you don't out-train a deficit. What this programme does is protect the 40.7kg of skeletal muscle you've already built while that happens, by keeping the strength lifts progressing (Upper A, Lower A) even while Upper B and Lower B run in more of a maintenance-volume mode. Don't be surprised if the bar numbers rise slower during the leanest weeks — that's expected, not a sign the programme's not working.
🏀
The dunk goal
Vertical power work sits in both lower days on purpose rather than as a separate "vert day" — Lower A keeps it light (approach jump practice, skill-focused), Lower B is where the real max-effort jump work happens once the posterior chain and knees are warmed through squats and Nordics. A year is a realistic timeline for this if the jump work stays consistent, not just the lifting.
