The AI layer, specced (15 Sep) — still Phase 6, built last
Home-LLM, not a from-scratch build. A Home Assistant component made exactly for this — plugs into your existing architecture as both the conversation agent (voice/chat) and inside automations (decision-making), so "assistant + automation" is one component, not two separate projects.
Local vs. hosted is no longer a hard fork. Home-LLM ships small purpose-built models (Llama 3.2 3B, Gemma 270M) light enough to run on Raspberry Pi-level hardware — so the spare desktop almost certainly runs it locally for free, whatever its specs turn out to be. Checking the specs (r4) still matters for whether it can also run a bigger general model for richer conversation.
Recommended setup: hybrid. Local model handles the bulk — device commands, simple automation calls — free, private, works offline. A cheap hosted model (GPT-4o-mini class, ~$0.15/$0.60 per million tokens as of Aug 2026) wired in only as a fallback for complex or free-form chat. At that pricing, even heavy personal use runs pennies to a couple of pounds a month — worth checking current rates again nearer the time.
Being honest about "learns patterns": a system that genuinely learns your habits with no rules is a much bigger, open-ended ML project than fits here — out of scope for now. What's realistic: standard Home Assistant automations built around your actual routine (night shift → climate/lighting by time of day, Xbox/PC wake), with Home-LLM layered on top to make case-by-case calls using live sensor data within those rules, plus handle the conversational side. That's genuinely "assistant + automation" without it becoming a research project.
Stays last in the build order — it needs Home Assistant live with real devices and entities exposed before there's anything for it to control. The difference now: Phase 6 isn't a placeholder anymore, it's a specced component ready to wire in when you get there.

Ways to make money — the actual focus
Royal Mail is out (25 Sep). Its fixed 20–40 hrs/week shift block clashes with playing for the basketball team. Was the confirmed bridge; now there's a genuine income gap until something else is running.
The real target: £2k/month (from the goals sheet) on fewer hours than Evri's ~38.5/week. That's above Evri's own ~£1,830/month, so it needs a meaningfully higher hourly rate, not more shifts.
Tutoring (Maths, Computer Science, Economics — your own A-level subjects). A student tutor without QTS realistically starts around £20–35/hr on Tutorful, MyTutor or First Tutors. At £25/hr, hitting £2k/month takes ~18.5 hrs/week — still under half Evri's hours. Most under-18 students require a DBS check first, which can take weeks — worth starting that now if this is the direction.
Wider platform list, researched 15 Sep: join now — PMT Education (18% commission, the lowest of any platform checked, but wants an enhanced DBS + strong A-level grades — the DBS you're already starting covers this) and Superprof (0% commission, keep 100% of what you set, no vetting bar, but you're competing in an open marketplace from zero reviews). Apply later, once Tutorful/PMT reviews exist — The Profs (£45–100/hr, but a real interview + trial lesson to pass) and Tutor Hunt (flexible, but ~33% commission eats a lot of it). Lower priority — GoStudent (pay works out lower per hour once you do the maths on their lesson bundles) and Preply (often USD-priced, 33% commission for new tutors, dominated by language tutoring). Not yet an option — Spires explicitly requires 2 years of paid tutoring experience; volunteering doesn't count, so this is a revisit-in-a-year platform, not a now one.
Filling the gap Royal Mail left, researched 25 Sep — needs zero fixed rota: gig delivery (Deliveroo, Uber Eats, Just Eat) — log on and off whenever, so it genuinely works around a changing basketball schedule; UK riders report roughly £10–14/hr before costs, and a bike needs its own food-delivery insurance (a legal requirement, and a real added cost to weigh in). Leeds Beckett SU casual roles (bar/event/venue staff via lbsu.mystaffsavvy.com) are built around student timetables, so likely more basketball-friendly than a normal retail or hospitality rota — worth checking live listings directly since pay/hours weren't published on the jobs page itself. Backup, less flexible: retail (~£10–12/hr, Trinity Leeds, Kirkgate Market, supermarkets) and hospitality (~£9–15/hr plus tips) still beat a job with zero flexibility, just with more of a fixed rota than delivery or SU work.
Video editing — not a skill yet, being learned for the new social media. Not an income lane right now. Worth building it through the social media itself first — real reps, a real portfolio — before pricing it as freelance work. Junior rates once ready run £15–25/hr, or £20–50 per short-form video, for reference.
Tutoring is still the near-term answer — no new skill needed, just the DBS lead time. Gig delivery or SU work can cover the gap while tutoring bookings ramp up. Video editing is the longer-term one, once the social media has actually built the skill up. None of this is locked in yet.
