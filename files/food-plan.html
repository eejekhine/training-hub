Your targets

Calories: about 2,450 a day. Mifflin-St Jeor puts your resting burn near 1,940 kcal. At about 1.5 times that for activity, maintenance is around 2,900 kcal (range 2,700 to 3,100). Eating roughly 450 below should lose 0.3 to 0.5 kg a week, which fits your 87–88 kg goal for mid-December.

Protein: about 150 g a day, or 1.6 g per kg, to hold your muscle while cutting and support the 100 kg bench goal. The default week lands a little under that to keep the basket cheap. See the upgrade below.

After about 14 Dec, when you are near 87–88 kg, slow the pace to about 0.2 kg a week for the 80 kg goal: add roughly 200 kcal a day (an extra 60 g of dry rice at lunch costs about £0.75 a week).

Batch cooking

Lunches for the week come from two cook sessions of about 2.5 hours in total, and dinners are quick. If you swap a batch lunch, only cook the boxes you still need.

  1. Shopping day. Freeze two of the three packs of thighs in portions, and move each portion to the fridge the night before you cook it.
  2. Sunday, about 75 minutes. Roast 800 g of thighs, cook the rice and peas, and pack 3 lunch boxes. Fridge the Monday box and freeze the other two once cool.
  3. Wednesday evening, about 90 minutes. Cook the chicken and bean stew with rice and pack 4 lunch boxes. Fridge the Thursday box and freeze the other three once cool.

Food safety. Cool rice and chicken within about an hour, then fridge or freezer. UK guidance is to eat cooked rice within a day and other leftovers within two, which is why only the first box stays in the fridge. Reheat until steaming hot all the way through, and check the current NHS advice if unsure.

Tuning it as you go

  • Weigh each morning after the toilet and before eating, and compare the weekly average, not single days.
  • If the average has not moved after 2 weeks, swap the milk and oat bowl snack for a lighter one (tuna on toast is 267 kcal).
  • If you lose more than 0.7 kg a week, or your lifts and sleep drop, add 60 g of dry rice at lunch for about 200 kcal.
  • More protein for about £3 a week: buy a second pack of 15 eggs and add 2 boiled eggs to the snack on four days.
  • Around training: have the toast and peanut butter or the oat bowl 1 to 2 hours before, and dinner within two hours after.

The white chocolate cookie

Your Sainsbury's favourite is 192 kcal and 2 g protein. This homemade one is about 170 kcal and 3 g protein, and costs around 15p instead of 23 to 30p. In Swap, pick it for any snack slot. Nutrition is approximate and the prices of flour, butter and sugar are my estimates.

How often: 2 to 3 a week. The plan already uses nearly all of your calories, so a cookie has to replace a snack, not sit on top of it. Three a week is about 510 kcal, roughly 16% of your weekly cut; one a day would eat about 40% and slow the cut. It has almost no protein, so on cookie days check you still reach about 150 g. Best after training.
