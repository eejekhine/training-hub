SHIFT HOOPER
Night Shift Edition · Pre-Shift Training
SHIFT
HOOPER
Built for training before an 8-hour heavy lifting night shift. Shorter sessions, lower fatigue, mobility and back health baked into every session. You go to work feeling better, not wrecked.
⚠️ PRE-SHIFT RULES
Sessions cap at 45–60 min. No leg day before shift — your legs work all night. Push and Pull are fine but volume is lower than your normal hooper programme. Nothing here should leave you exhausted — if it does, drop a set next time.
🤲 HAND PAIN — READ THE HANDS TAB
All exercises in this programme are selected or modified to reduce wrist and grip stress. Straps are used on all pull movements. No direct forearm or grip training. See the Hands tab for daily care protocol.
🔄 COMPATIBLE WITH HOOPER PROGRAMME
This is a lighter version of Push / Pull / Legs. Run it instead of your main hooper programme on shift weeks. On days off, you can return to the full hooper or couples sessions without issue.

HAND & WRIST CARE PROTOCOL
🤲 WHY YOUR HANDS HURT
You're brand new to repetitive gripping at volume. The tendons and joints in your hands haven't adapted yet. This is normal — but if ignored it becomes tendinitis or worse. The protocol below should show improvement within 2–3 weeks.
DURING SHIFT
🧤
Compression Gloves
Wear compression gloves on shift. They reduce joint inflammation during repetitive gripping and keep the hands warm — cold hands are stiffer and more prone to injury. Get a pair that's snug but not cutting circulation.
🔄
Grip Variation
Don't grip everything the same way all shift if you can help it. Alternate between a full grip and a hook grip where possible. Give your fingers small breaks during any standing or waiting moments.
AFTER SHIFT
🧊
Ice — 10 Minutes
Ice the knuckles and wrists for 10 minutes when you get home. Wrap ice in a cloth — don't apply directly to skin. This reduces the inflammation that's building up during the shift before it compounds.
💊
Ibuprofen Gel
Apply ibuprofen gel (Voltarol or similar) to the joints after shift — not before. Masking pain before shift means you won't notice if you're making something worse. After only.
DAILY — 5 MINUTES
🤲
Wrist Circles
10 slow circles each direction, each wrist. Do this when you wake up and before bed. Keep the movement smooth and full range — don't push into pain, just move through the range you have.
✋
Finger Extensions
Spread your fingers as wide as possible, hold 5 seconds, relax. Repeat 10 times each hand. This counters the constant gripping position and stretches the tendons on the palm side of the hand.
🙏
Prayer Stretch
Press palms together in front of your chest, fingers pointing up. Slowly lower the hands toward your waist while keeping palms pressed together. Hold at the point of stretch for 30 seconds. Targets the wrist flexors that get overworked gripping.
👇
Reverse Prayer Stretch
Backs of hands together, fingers pointing down, same motion. 30 seconds. Hits the extensors on the back of the wrist and forearm. Do both prayer stretches together — 1 minute total.
⚠️
When to see a doctor
If pain doesn't improve after 3 weeks, gets significantly worse, or you notice swelling that doesn't go down after ice — get it looked at. Early repetitive strain caught early is nothing. Ignored for months it becomes a real problem.
AT THE GYM
🔗
Use Straps on All Pull Movements
Lat pulldown, cable row, pull-ups — use straps every time. Your back will still get a full workout. Your hands get a rest. This is not a weakness, it's basic injury management.
💪
Cable Curls Over Barbell Curls
Cable curls allow a more neutral wrist position and less torque on the joints than a fixed barbell. Hammer curls with DBs are also fine. Avoid straight barbell curls until the hands have settled down.
🚫
No Direct Grip or Forearm Training
Your job is already training your grip and forearms at high volume every single night. Adding more at the gym is how you get tendinitis. Skip all forearm-specific work until you've been on the job at least 6–8 weeks and the pain has fully resolved.
